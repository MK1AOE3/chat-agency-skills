# Path: parent and teen

## Focus

Learn together through consent, curiosity, and clear boundaries. This path is designed primarily for parents or guardians with teenagers. Follow current platform age requirements; younger children need closer adult supervision.

The activity is not a reason to read every private chat or demand personal disclosures.

## Create a five-point agreement

Discuss and write down:

1. **Purpose:** What kinds of tasks may we use AI for?
2. **Privacy:** What information and files will we never upload?
3. **Time:** When and for how long will we use it?
4. **Human help:** Which topics require a trusted adult or qualified professional?
5. **Stop signal:** What word or action means either person wants to pause without debate?

Revisit the agreement as the learner's age, needs, and school rules change.

## A 25-minute shared activity

Use a neutral task, such as planning a fictional event or comparing two study methods.

### 1. Set together — 4 minutes

Agree on a finish line. Let the teen phrase the goal in their own words.

### 2. Tell safely — 4 minutes

Review the prompt for unnecessary identifying details. The parent can model redaction using a fictional example.

### 3. Explore — 5 minutes

Ask for two options and one question. Discuss which option fits your values and constraints; do not let the AI make the family decision.

### 4. Examine — 6 minutes

Each person chooses one claim or assumption to check. Compare what the AI said with an authoritative source where relevant.

### 5. Redirect or stop — 4 minutes

Let the teen give the correction. Practise the stop signal and end on time even if the AI suggests more steps.

### 6. Reflect — 2 minutes

Each person answers:

- What did you control well?
- What should people—not AI—decide?
- What should we change in our agreement?

## Helpful parent language

- “Show me how you would redirect this answer.”
- “What would you like to check before trusting it?”
- “We can stop without finishing.”
- “Would you like help from me, a teacher, or someone else?”

Avoid using the activity to shame mistakes, secretly monitor, or force disclosure of difficult experiences. If a young person may be in immediate danger, use appropriate local emergency or crisis support and stay connected with a trusted person.
