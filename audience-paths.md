# Audience paths

Select the nearest track and adapt to the learner's actual needs. Do not infer age, school status, or family situation.

## Ages 16–20

Emphasize personal boundaries, study and early-work tasks, privacy, emotional pace, and independent verification. Use realistic examples such as planning revision, improving a draft, preparing for an interview, or organising a project. Do not let the AI make identity, relationship, or life decisions for the learner.
Practice sequence: set a finish line → redact private details → ask for one small step → challenge a confident answer → save a personal prompt.

## High school

Follow current school and teacher rules for AI use. Teach AI as a tutor and feedback tool, not a way to bypass learning. Prefer explanations, hints, self-testing, and draft feedback over ready-to-submit work. Require independent source checks and disclosure of assistance where rules require it.

Practice sequence: request hints → ask for a quiz → check a source → improve the learner's own draft → reflect on what the learner can now do unaided.

## University

Use research questions, source evaluation, comparison tables, coding tests, project handoffs, and argument critique. Distinguish AI-generated leads from verified evidence. Follow course, institution, citation, data, and research-ethics requirements. Never invent references or treat generated citations as verified.

Practice sequence: narrow a research question → request a source-check plan → critique assumptions → test generated code or analysis → export decisions and open questions.

## Parent and teen

Coach a shared activity rather than surveillance or interrogation. Agree on purpose, privacy, time limits, topics requiring adult help, and a stop signal. A parent models curiosity and verification; the teen retains age-appropriate voice and choice. Do not demand disclosure of every private conversation. Follow platform age requirements and use closer adult supervision for younger children.

Practice sequence: create the agreement → solve a neutral task together → let the teen redirect the AI → review one privacy choice → discuss when to ask a person for help.
