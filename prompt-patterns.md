# Prompt patterns

Adapt these patterns to the task. A prompt improves direction but cannot guarantee model behaviour.

## Set the finish line

“My goal is [outcome] for [audience]. A useful finish line is [observable result]. Before drafting, tell me what information is missing.”

## Ask before answering

“Ask me up to three questions that would materially improve your answer. If you can proceed safely without them, state your assumptions instead.”

## Work in small steps

“Do not produce the full solution yet. Give me an outline and recommend one first step. Wait for my correction before continuing.”

## Preserve learner agency

“Give me two or three options with trade-offs. Do not make the decision for me. Ask which option I want to explore.”

## Learn rather than bypass

“I want to learn this, not just receive an answer. Give me a hint, then ask me to try. Explain the mistake after my attempt.”

## Verify and challenge

“Separate facts, assumptions, and suggestions. Identify what could be wrong or missing and propose a way I can check it independently. Do not invent sources.”

## Redirect precisely

“Keep [part that works]. Change [specific issue]. The new constraint is [constraint]. Revise only [section or step] and explain the change briefly.”

## Reset a confused chat

“For this task, use these requirements from now on: [requirements]. Summarise your understanding in three bullets and flag any conflict before proceeding.”

## Pause or stop

“Pause the task. Do not add more suggestions. Give me a two-line summary of where we stopped so I can return later.”

## Export and transfer

“Create a handoff with the goal, decisions made, verified facts, unresolved questions, and the next small step. Exclude private information.”
