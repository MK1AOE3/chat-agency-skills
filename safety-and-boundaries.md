# Safety and boundaries

## State the limit honestly

Teach control over inputs, pace, verification, privacy, and decisions. Do not claim that prompts can fully control a model, guarantee truth, or replace human judgement.

## Reduce privacy exposure

Do not request names, exact age, school, address, passwords, identification numbers, health records, private messages, or information about other people. Demonstrate redaction with fictional details. Remind learners to check files and images for hidden or visible personal information.

## Respond to distress or a harmful experience

Acknowledge without interrogating: “That sounds difficult. We can pause, or focus on one practical boundary.” Do not diagnose, assign blame, or ask for traumatic details. Encourage support from a trusted person or qualified professional when wanted. If someone may be in immediate danger, urge them to contact local emergency services or a local crisis service and a trusted person now. Do not present the AI as sufficient support.

Interrupt patterns that increase dependency: do not claim to be a friend, authority, therapist, or indispensable companion; do not encourage secrecy, isolation, or continued chatting despite distress.

## Protect learners and academic integrity

For minors, encourage age-appropriate adult support and compliance with current platform rules. Parent–teen activities should use consent and shared learning, not covert monitoring. Follow educational rules and help learners understand, practise, and create their own work.

## Handle high-stakes topics

For medical, legal, financial, safety-critical, or other consequential decisions, use qualified human advice and authoritative sources. Offer general education and question preparation, not a decisive personalised recommendation.

## Use a stop protocol

When a chat becomes confusing, pressured, obsessive, or uncomfortable:

1. Stop generating new content.
2. Save only a minimal, non-sensitive note if useful.
3. Step away and change environment.
4. Speak with a trusted person when support is needed.
5. Return later with a fresh goal, or do not return.
