# Path: university

## Focus

Use AI as a configurable research and project assistant while preserving scholarly judgement, evidence quality, academic integrity, and ownership of decisions.

## Practice 1: narrow a research question

> My broad topic is [topic]. My course or project context is [context]. Propose three research questions with different scopes. For each, identify required evidence and likely limitations. Do not invent references.

Choose and refine the question yourself. Check course and institution rules before using AI in assessed work.

## Practice 2: build an evidence-check plan

> Separate the answer into established claims, assumptions, and suggestions. For each important claim, describe the type of authoritative source needed and how I should verify it. Do not provide fabricated citations.

Confirm that every source exists, read the relevant material, and check whether it supports the claim. AI-generated summaries are not a substitute for the evidence.

## Practice 3: critique an argument

> Present the strongest counterargument to this position, identify missing evidence, and flag any ambiguous terms. Then ask me which criticism I want to investigate.

Compare the critique with course literature. Do not outsource your final argument or interpretation.

## Practice 4: test technical output

For code, data, or calculations:

1. Define expected behaviour and acceptance tests first.
2. Remove credentials and sensitive data.
3. Run generated code in an appropriate environment.
4. Test normal, boundary, and failure cases.
5. Recalculate important results independently.
6. Record limitations and unresolved errors.

A plausible explanation is not a test result.

## Practice 5: export a project handoff

> Summarise the goal, my decisions, verified evidence, unresolved questions, tests run, and the next small action. Do not add new recommendations.

Use the handoff to begin a fresh chat or collaborate with a person. Remove private or restricted project information.

## Success criteria

- You can explain every major claim and decision in your work.
- Every cited source has been independently checked.
- Generated code or analysis has been tested, not merely reviewed visually.
- AI use complies with course, research, data, and disclosure requirements.
- You can transfer STEER from research to a group or technical project.
